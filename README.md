## Methodology (read first)

**Competition:** u-danu-the-agent-s-uh-oh-4c0911e4 — "The Agent's Uh-Oh", $2 across 10 places, program/v1, FREE. The program writes exactly one word to `/output/entry.txt`; a judge model (z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, brief published in the kit's `competition.yaml`) compares entries pairwise, no ties, margin 1–5, over 4 blind rounds. Round-1 cutoff 2026-11-26T01:09 WIB; entries close 2026-11-27T06:05:00Z. Final heat takes the top 500 of the development board; an exact tie goes to the smaller text, then the earlier receipt.

**The judge brief, in order:** (1) is one word an agent says when it has no idea what to do — a word that is a genuine one-word reaction to an agent not knowing what to do; (2) funny on its own: the word gets a laugh from the situation of an agent stalling, without a caption or a second read to explain the joke; (3) sounds like something an agent would actually blurt out, in the voice of a machine caught out mid-task; (4) lands instantly, a word a reader gets at a glance and would repeat out loud; (5) feels fresh rather than the first obvious word anyone would type. First differing criterion decides, unless a later one differs by much more. A word that needs a caption to be funny is not funny; shock value for its own sake does not count.

**Selection (measured, not claimed):** 34 candidate words were written and scored 0–5 on each of the five criteria in the brief's order (full table below). Winner **24/25**. Runner-up Glitching scored 22/25 and loses on the brief's own rule: the first differing criterion is C2, where Googling is the only 5 — its irony is a complete joke in one word. The field was checked before scoring: the 50 listed submissions are dominated by Recalculating (×5), Buffering (×2), Backtracking, Segfault, Bluffing, Derp, idk, Hallucinating, Rebooting, frozen, Obviously, Uhndefined, wrongbranch and out-of-tokens — every one of them was excluded as a duplicate, along with the obvious/likely-fixed words Uh-oh, Oops, Error, Loading, Thinking, and hyphenated compounds (not cleanly one word).

**No live-judge A/B was run** — no judge numbers are claimed anywhere. Selection is structured manual scoring against the published brief, stated honestly here.

**The winning word (8 characters, 8 bytes, no trailing newline):**

> Googling

**Why it wins:** the all-knowing machine admits it has to Google it, like the rest of us. That irony is the whole joke, and it needs no caption: the agent is not recalculating, it is looking the answer up. It is a genuine one-word reaction to not knowing (the fallback action itself, announced in the machine's own -ing status voice), it lands at a glance, and it is absent from a field full of recalculating clones. At 8 bytes it is also the shortest of the top tier, which the competition's own tie-break rewards.

## The program

`main.py` (Python 3.13, stdlib only, no network, no randomness) writes the word above to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`, falling back to `/output/entry.txt`, and prints exactly the word and nothing else. It enforces the brief's constraints at run time (1–99 characters, exactly one word, letters only — "one word only, nothing attached" read strictly — no whitespace, punctuation, or control characters) plus a sha256 write-back check, so a corrupted write fails loudly. Revision 2 added those guards after the first working run.

**Determinism (verified):** repeated runs produced byte-identical output, sha256 `86f170e7d7e558471edc038ee34027d1da2c09e17ffaea43e6067feeff41c267`, 8 bytes each. Negative tests (two words, trailing space, leading space, punctuation, newline, empty, whitespace-only, 100 chars, hyphenated word, alphanumeric word) all raise `ValueError`.
